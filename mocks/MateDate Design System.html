Design System

The kit behind the flirting-as-chess engine. Warm paper, decisive ink, one rosy accent, and a chess vocabulary that grades every move. Built to render one recognizable, shareable card across every mode.

Do & don't

✓ Do
Keep the verdict funny and the framing self-improvement. "That 'idk lol' was a −3.4 Blunder — here's the Brilliant line." Anonymize before sharing. Use !! only for genuinely brilliant moves.
✕ Don't
Never rate a person's face or appearance. Never imply chess.com affiliation or reuse its exact badge artwork. Never present a bot as a real human. Don't market to teens.

05

Buttons

Pill-shaped, chunky, tactile. The primary rosy button carries a hard bottom shadow that depresses on press — the whole product should feel like it clicks.

06

Move classification badges

The heart of the engine. Each message maps its eval delta to one of six tiers. The glyph carries the meaning; the color reinforces it. !! and ?? are reserved for the extremes.

!!Brilliant +2.4 !Great +1.6 ✓Good +0.6 ?!Inaccuracy −0.7 ?Mistake −1.8 ??Blunder −3.4

Thresholds (tune on data): Brilliant ≥ +2.0 & non-obvious · Great +1.0–2.0 · Good +0.2–1.0 · Inaccuracy −0.2 to −1.0 · Mistake −1.0 to −2.5 · Blunder ≤ −2.5.

Anatomy

  • Header — wordmark + big accuracy % (the shareable brag).
  • Eval strip — the tug-of-war at a glance.
  • Thread — 2–4 re-rendered bubbles with move tags. Never the original screenshot — no names, avatars, or source-app UI.
  • Locked best move — blurred, the primary paywall lever. Free users share the verdict; the fix is paid.
  • Footer — rizz rating + ELO delta, plus the anonymization trust badge.
✓ Privacy by construction
Cards are re-rendered from cleaned, redacted text. Raw screenshots are processed in memory and dropped — they never reach the card. Market as pseudonymized, not "fully anonymous."
